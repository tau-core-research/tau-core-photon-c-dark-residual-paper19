## Abstract

This draft develops a Tau Core research line in which the photon, special relativity, gravitational lensing, and dark-sector residuals are treated through a common readout-language. The central claim is not that standard physics is false, nor that dark matter is disproven. Rather, the paper proposes a structured hypothesis: the observed 3+1D physical world may be a stable readout of a deeper parent-morphological background, and some residuals currently modeled phenomenologically as dark-sector components may contain morphology-linked contributions to the effective 4D geometry.

The draft introduces the **Photon-C Module**, where the photon is interpreted as a null-compatible electromagnetic phase response of the Tau Core parent field, and the speed of light `c` is interpreted as the invariant causal closure rate of the 3+1D readout. It then develops a **Relativity Module**, where Lorentz invariance, proper time, mass, and `E = mc²` are described as internal consistency properties of the readout. This is followed by a **Gravity / Dark Residual Module**, where gravity is treated as a metric/geometric state response of the 4D readout, and lensing is interpreted as a null-probe of readout geometry.

Finally, the paper defines the **Tau Core Dark Residual Protocol** from v1 to v4. The protocol tests whether residuals remaining after standard baryonic and halo modeling contain pre-registered morphology-, filament-boundary-, break-radius-, or null/time split structure. The protocol is designed to be falsifiable: if residuals disappear under standard controls, randomization, halo-family variation, baryonic uncertainty marginalization, survey-systematics checks, or out-of-sample validation failure, the dark-residual module is not supported.

```text

particle side = local readout closure event
```

For a photon:

```text
photon propagation = open electromagnetic compatibility
photon detection   = local 4D closure event
```

This avoids treating the photon as a little classical ball while preserving its event-like detection.

### 2.4 The role of `c`

A photon travels at `c` because it is the null-compatible electromagnetic response mode that carries no local rest stabilization.


This must not be interpreted as a classical ether or a digital frame-rate. The correct interpretation is geometric and relational:

## 3. Relativity Module

### 3.1 Relativity as readout self-consistency

The Tau Core reading of special relativity:

> Special relativity expresses the internal self-consistency of the 3+1D readout under different internal observer slicings.

Every observer, measuring device, and photon is part of the same readout. Therefore all internal observers measure the same invariant causal closure rate `c`.

```text
observer ∈ R₄
apparatus ∈ R₄
photon ∈ R₄
c = invariant of R₄
```

### 3.2 Light cone as compatibility domain

In standard language, the light cone defines causal reachability. In Tau Core:

> The light cone is the local consistency domain of the 4D readout.

Events outside the light cone are not forbidden because “light is too slow” in a naive sense, but because the readout cannot establish locally consistent causal closure outside that domain.

### 3.3 Lorentz transformations

A Lorentz transformation is interpreted as:

> A self-consistent reslicing of the same 4D readout between moving internal reference frames.

The invariant interval:

```text
s² = c²t² - x² - y² - z²
```

is read as:

> the invariant causal stability measure of the 4D readout.

Different observers can disagree on spatial and temporal decomposition, but not on the underlying causal closure structure.

### 3.4 Proper time

Proper time is interpreted as:

> the internal stabilization parameter of a locally rest-stabilized readout mode.

Thus:

```text
photon:
    null trajectory
    no rest frame
    no proper-time accumulation

massive particle:
    timelike trajectory
    local rest stabilization
    proper time exists
```

The standard relation:

```text
Δτ = Δt √(1 - v²/c²)
```

becomes:

> as a readout trajectory approaches the null boundary, its locally stabilized proper-time component tends to zero.

### 3.5 Mass as local readout stabilization

Tau Core proposes the interpretation:

> Mass is local readout-stabilization inertia.

A massive particle is not merely “stuff”; it is energy stabilized as a persistent, proper-time-bearing local readout mode.

```text
photon = energy as propagating null phase response
massive particle = energy as local stabilized readout
```

### 3.6 Energy relation

The standard relation:

```text
E² = p²c² + m²c⁴
```

receives the following readout interpretation:

```text
E = total readout-stabilization capacity
p = directed spatial readout compatibility
m = local rest-stabilization inertia
c = causal closure ratio
```

For a photon:

```text

```

For a rest-stabilized object:

```text
p = 0 → E = mc²
```

The proposed interpretive sentence is:

> Mass is readout-energy locally retained relative to the light-cone boundary.

This is not a replacement of relativity; it is a Tau Core reading of why the same invariant `c` links null propagation and mass-energy conversion.

## 12. Risks and Weak Points

The Tau Core programme is vulnerable to several criticisms:

1. **Over-flexibility:** if every residual can be called readout, the framework becomes unfalsifiable.
2. **No independent equation:** without a constrained `Θμν^τ`, the theory risks being only vocabulary.
3. **Standard absorption:** better baryon/halo/systematics models may remove the signal.
4. **Look-elsewhere effects:** many possible morphology/filament features can generate accidental correlations.
5. **Lensing/dynamics complexity:** null/time split may be dominated by ordinary modeling challenges.
6. **Lack of Born-rule or quantum derivation:** the quantum branch still requires deeper formalization.

The TCDR programme is designed specifically to reduce these risks through pre-registration and controls.
